# The marketing logic

**Written 2026-09-10.** This is the reasoning layer — *why* the machine is shaped
this way. Tactics, calendars and copy come after, and only where they follow from
something here.

**Status honesty.** This rests on a complete newsletter audit, a partial channel
inventory, and one benchmark teardown. Instagram post-level data is missing and
website analytics do not exist yet. Where a conclusion would flip if those came back
differently, §11 says so. It is written now rather than after the research because
the September gate does not wait.

---

## 1. The constraints that actually decide this

Not context — constraints. Every one of these removes options.

| Constraint | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **ODDfest 2027 has a September 2026 launch gate: 30 tickets minimum or wind down** | This is not a brand-building quarter. Marketing has ~weeks to produce a countable number. |
| **€260K gap; 8 open critical items in the ODDfest/NNW domain** | No budget, no new hires, and Ronny's attention is the scarcest input in the system. Anything that needs weekly production from a person who doesn't exist will not happen. |
| **1,514-person list at 60.9% open, silent 80 days** | The single best asset, and it is depreciating. |
| **Social has produced 16 newsletter subscribers, ever** — against 4,409 Instagram + 3,341 LinkedIn followers | The audience is real and it is ODD's biggest. It has simply never been connected to anything. A conversion problem, not an audience problem. |
| **No analytics** | We cannot optimise. We can only count things that count themselves — emails, tickets, members, replies. |
| **5,000+ participants, 500+ collaborators, 100+ partners** | Against a 1,514-person list that is 84% one import. |

That last row is the whole strategy.

## 2. The problem marketing is actually solving

**ODD does not have an awareness problem. It has a capture problem.**

More than 5,000 people have been in an ODD room. ODD can reach 1,514 of them, and
84% of those arrived in a single August 2025 import. Somewhere between three and four
thousand people have physically attended something ODD made and cannot be contacted
again.

Meanwhile the acquisition instinct — post more, grow the followers, launch the
podcast — points *outward*, at strangers. And the channels ODD already owns are
bigger than anyone assumed: 4,409 people follow `@oddfest.co`, 3,341 follow the
LinkedIn page, 3,626 follow Ronny. Between them they have delivered **16 newsletter
subscribers in two years**, because nothing on any of them has ever pointed anywhere
measurable.

The logic of this plan: **stop trying to reach new people until you can reach the
ones who already came.** Everything below serves that, and the growth work resumes
once the leak is closed.

## 3. The engine: the Room Loop

ODD's real structural advantage is not the festival. It is that **ODD runs rooms
year-round** — 32 events in a four-month window of the calendar snapshot alone:
Open Doors, Oddly Creative Lunch, member gatherings, Help Me Help You, Kaleidoscope,
Coffee on the House, ODDspace launches.

Most festivals have one moment a year and must manufacture content in between. ODD
has a moment most weeks and manufactures nothing from any of them.

Right now a room produces: an experience, and then nothing.

It should produce three things, every time, by default:

```
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │                                                      │
   ▼                                                      │
 ROOM  ──►  CAPTURE  ──►  RECORD  ──►  REACH  ────────────┘
 an event   everyone in   one artifact  personal accounts
 happens    the room is   out of the    first, brand
            on the list,  room          second, newsletter
            tagged by                   as the destination
            which room
```

**Capture.** Nobody leaves a room untagged. Which room they were in *is* the
segmentation — artist, member, delegate, partner, ticket-buyer. ODDletters currently
has none, so every send goes to all 1,514 identically. Slush segments at signup into
Startup / Investor / Media / Other; that is free and we don't do it.

**Record.** One artifact per room. Not a content plan — a rule. A photo set, a
90-second film, one quote, one paragraph. The Buildspace finding holds: five short
films totalling 9.2 minutes beat 24 lectures totalling 14.67 hours. Volume is not
the lever; placement is.

**Reach.** Covered in §5.

**Return.** The artifact recruits the next room. This is the only loop in the plan
that compounds, and it costs nothing that isn't already happening.

The test of whether this is working is not reach. It is: **what percentage of people
in the room end up reachable afterwards?** Today that number is unmeasured and
almost certainly under 20%.

## 4. What the audience actually wants — from ODD's own data

The four worst-clicking newsletter sends in ODD's history are all lineup
announcements (2.00%, 2.07%, 3.01%, 5.00%). The best are "Everything You Need to
Know" (9.46%), "Hello Again, We've Been Busy" (8.88%), "Thank You" (8.56%) and
"New Ways to Get Involved" (8.41%).

Opens stay high across both — 54–64%. **People open for the news and act on the
usefulness.** The programming is what they are curious about; the practical next
step is what they move on.

Buildspace found the same thing independently: person-led posts beat programme-led
posts 2.5×, and the programme was never the subject.

So the content hierarchy is:

1. **A person** — one named human doing something specific
2. **A useful thing** — how to take part, what you need to know, what happens next
3. **A moment** — proof that something real happened
4. **The programme** — last, and always attached to one of the above

**With one split by medium.** ODD's best Instagram post is gratitude (121 likes);
its worst is a feedback form (21). On email it inverts — "Everything You Need to
Know" clicked 9.46% while lineup drops clicked 2%. So:

> **Instagram carries the feeling. Email carries the instruction.**

Posting admin to Instagram spends attention cheaply. Sending pure hype to a 60%-open
email wastes the best asset ODD owns. Both are currently happening.

This inverts how ODD has been marketing itself. Lineup drops were treated as the main
event and they are the weakest content ODD produces.

## 5. Channel doctrine

The benchmarks are unambiguous, and they do not split by brand hierarchy — they split
by **audience type**:

| Org | Instagram | LinkedIn | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|---|
| Horst | 109K | **63** | A culture brand can win completely on Instagram and be absent from LinkedIn. |
| Nuits Sonores / Arty Farty | 101K (festival) | 19,479 (**roof brand**) | The institutional entity carries LinkedIn. The culture brand carries Instagram. |
| Slush | 31K | **110,958** | A B2B event inverts it entirely. |

ODD is both. That is the actual complication, and it resolves cleanly:

| Channel | Who speaks | Audience | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** — `@oddfest.co`, 4,409, the biggest channel | ODDfest, ODDspace — product brands | Creatives, artists, participants, members | The culture. Rooms, people, moments. **Carries the feeling, not the instruction.** |
| **LinkedIn** | **People first**, ODD masterbrand second | Partners, delegates, funders, press, ODDagency clients | Credibility and revenue. |
| **Newsletter** | ODD | Everyone, segmented | The destination. Not a channel — the place the others point. |
| **Discord (ODDones, 315)** | Members | Insiders | Retention, not acquisition. Currently scoped to ODDfest; should be ODD. |
| **YouTube (24 subs)** | — | — | A file cabinet for embeds. Not a channel. Do not resource it. |
| **TikTok** | — | — | **Closed until a named person owns it.** The account currently linked from the live site does not resolve. |

### The uncomfortable part: people, not pages

Three independent channels, one result:

| Evidence | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Ronny's LinkedIn post on ODDfest 2025: **533 reactions, 70 comments**. Nothing on the 3,341-follower company page approaches it. | ~10× |
| Ronny's Instagram post: **113 likes on 3,626 followers (3.1%)** vs `@oddfest.co`'s ~1.1% median. Third-party posts about ODD drew 114 and 135. | **2.5–3×** |
| Buildspace: third-person participant stories, **13,162 likes vs 5,287**. | **2.5×** |

**So LinkedIn is not a page. It is a squad.** Eleven core team members, plus the
speakers, artists and members who already post voluntarily. The brand page exists to
be tagged and to hold the canonical version — it is not the primary surface.

This is also the cheapest thing in the plan, and the hardest, because it requires
other people to do something. The ODDference LinkedIn kit and Canva templates already
exist in `wiki/projects/social-kits/`. They were built. What is missing is the
follow-through, and that is a coordination problem, not a content problem.

## 6. The newsletter is the spine

At 60.9% median open, ODDletters reaches roughly 920 people who genuinely read it.
That is a larger *attentive* audience than any social channel ODD owns.

Four changes, in order of value:

1. **Segment on capture.** Which room they came from. Everything else depends on it.
2. **Publish it on `allthingsodd.co`.** Median 15 web views per issue means every
   newsletter ODD has ever written is invisible to Google, to a partner doing due
   diligence, and to the site itself. There is currently **no searchable body of ODD
   writing on the open web.** Slush's Soaked understood this — it called itself
   *media* and used the newsletter as the subscription mechanism, not the product.
3. **Fix attribution before relaunch.** 92% "direct" means we relaunch and learn
   nothing. UTM every outbound link first. This is a one-hour job.
4. **The re-open send is the highest-stakes email in the plan.** 80 days of silence.
   It must not be a rebrand announcement — "we changed our name" is about ODD.
   Based on §4, it should be about what happened and what someone can do next.

**Cadence: monthly.** Soaked ran monthly with a full editorial team. ODD does not
have one. A cadence that breaks is worse than a slower one that holds.

## 7. Podcast logic

The reason to do a podcast is **not audience**. At ODD's scale a new podcast reaches
approximately nobody for a year.

The reason is **access**. "Come on our podcast" is a legitimate, flattering,
low-cost reason to contact any partner, funder, artist or operator in the Nordics —
and ODD needs to be contacting those people anyway for €260K worth of reasons. The
episode is the byproduct; the relationship is the product.

That reframes every design decision:

- **Guests are chosen for who ODD needs to know**, not for download numbers.
- **It records in ODDspace**, because the room is already there and a fixed camera
  costs nothing per episode. This is Buildspace's off-season-teaching logic: publish
  what is already happening in a room you already pay for.
- **It interleaves with written content** rather than living in its own section —
  Soaked ran podcast episodes and articles in one stream tagged by type. A separate
  podcast section is a separate thing to maintain, and it is the thing that dies.
- **Undertow already exists** — a documentary interview series with Remu Shemeikka,
  pilot done with three guests, seven in the pipeline, pitch deck built. Starting a
  second interview format before that one is distributed would be a mistake worth
  naming out loud.

**Recommendation: do not launch a podcast in Q4 2026.** Distribute Undertow, run the
Room Loop, and revisit once the list is growing. This will be unpopular and it is
what the constraints say.

## 8. What we will not do, and why

Stating these so they don't get relitigated every month.

| Not doing | Why |
|---|---|
| Growing followers as a goal | 16 newsletter subscribers from all social, ever. The follower count is not connected to anything ODD needs. |
| Opening TikTok | No owner, no capacity, and the existing link is already broken. Adding a channel while five are underused is a way of feeling busy. |
| A new visual identity per edition (the OFFF model) | OFFF refreshes annually against a *fixed wordmark* and a 25-year-old reputation. ODD's recognition is too young to spend. |
| Paid acquisition | No budget, no analytics, and no measurable landing page. Money spent now is money spent blind. |
| Weekly anything | The cadence that breaks is worse than the slower one that holds. |

## 9. Measurement, given that we cannot measure

Until GA4 and Search Console work (blocker B16), track only things that count
themselves. Four numbers, monthly:

| Metric | Today | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| **Newsletter list, active** | 1,514 | The only compounding asset. |
| **Capture rate** — % of room attendees who end up on the list | unmeasured | The engine's health. If this doesn't move, nothing else matters. |
| **Revenue-countable actions** — tickets, memberships, ODDference pre-buys | — | The September gate is a ticket count, not a reach number. |
| **Replies and inbound** — DMs, emails, "can we talk" | unmeasured | The only honest early signal that the personal-account strategy is working. |

Vanity metrics are recorded in `intel/` because they are cheap and comparable. They
are not targets.

## 10. Sequencing

Against the real gates, not a generic funnel.

**Now → end of September — the gate.**
Not a brand launch. Re-open the list; convert the people who already came. The
ODDference 2027 early-access email to 267 previous attendees (target: 50 pre-buys ×
€350 = €17,500) is already an open loop and is the closest thing to revenue in the
system. Hit the 30-ticket gate or trigger the wind-down conversation honestly.

**October — the grant window.**
Taike Festival Grants and the SKR national round both run 1–30.10.2026. Marketing's
job in October is *evidence*: the impact report, the cumulative proof, the named
partners. This is content that serves an application, not an algorithm — and ODD
already built an Impact Report tool. Slush treats research output as marketing; ODD
treats it as reporting. Change that.

**November → February — the loop.**
Install the Room Loop properly. Every ODDspace event captures and records. Segment
the list. Publish the newsletter to the web. Get the personal-account squad running.
This is the quiet quarter where the machine gets built, and it is also when Buildspace
did its off-season teaching.

**March → the 2027 season.**
Only now does front-loaded campaign work make sense. Buildspace's shape: a long ramp
of roughly twenty weeks into the gate, then a deliberate drop. Films placed at
specific distances from the date — D−56, D−43, D−5, D+0, D+12 — not sprayed.

## 11. What would make this wrong

Named now, so they get checked rather than defended.

- **If D1 resolves to "All Things ODD is the roof brand"**, §5 needs rebuilding — the
  channel doctrine assumes ODD stays the masterbrand and products keep their names.
- ~~If `@oddfest.fi` turns out to be large and engaged…~~ **Resolved 2026-09-10, and it
  did change things.** The account is `@oddfest.co`, it has 4,409 followers, and the
  handle on the live site does not exist. The "social is not an acquisition channel"
  claim has been narrowed accordingly. The remaining risk is the reverse: the 12-post
  sample is small, and the other 228 could tell a different story.
- **If the September gate fails and ODDfest winds down**, the centre of gravity moves
  to ODDspace and ODDagency, and this plan needs re-pointing rather than editing.
- **If the August 2025 import turns out not to have a consent basis for email**, the
  list is smaller than 1,514 and the spine of this plan changes shape.
- **The Room Loop assumes ODDspace keeps running weekly events.** The YIT lease
  verification is still an open blocker.
