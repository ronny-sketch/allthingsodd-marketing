# The Room Loop — the operating procedure

The engine from [`00-marketing-logic.md`](00-marketing-logic.md) §3, written as
something a person can actually run.

**The premise:** ODD's real advantage is not the festival. It is that ODD runs rooms
year-round — 32 events in a four-month window of the calendar. Most festivals have
one moment a year and must manufacture content in between. ODD has a moment most
weeks and manufactures nothing from any of them.

**The gap this closes:** 5,000+ people have been in an ODD room. 1,514 are reachable.

---

## The loop

```
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │                                                            │
   ▼                                                            │
 ROOM ────► CAPTURE ────► RECORD ────► REACH ───────────────────┘
 an event   everyone is   one artifact  people first,
 happens    on the list,  out of the    then brand,
            tagged by     room          newsletter as
            which room                  the destination
```

Four steps. If a room does not produce all four, the room was a cost, not an asset.

---

## Step 1 — CAPTURE

**Rule: nobody leaves a room untagged.**

| Room type | Capture method | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| ODDspace event (Open Doors, Oddly Creative Lunch, Coffee on the House) | QR on the door and on the table → one-field signup | `participant` |
| Member gathering | Already known — tag from the membership record | `member` |
| ODDference | Ticket data | `delegate` |
| ODDfest | Ticket data | `participant` |
| Partner meeting | Manual, same day | `partner` |

**Minimum viable version:** a printed QR code that points at a Beehiiv form with the
segment pre-set in the URL. One per room type. Costs an afternoon, once.

**Why this is first:** everything else in the marketing plan degrades to "post more"
without it. It is also the only step that directly reverses the number that matters —
3,500 people who came and cannot be contacted.

## Step 2 — RECORD

**Rule: one artifact per room. One.**

Not a content plan — a constraint. Buildspace's five short films totalling 9.2
minutes drew 270,560 views; its 24 lectures totalling 14.67 hours drew 135,652.
Volume is not the lever.

Acceptable artifacts, in descending order of value:

| Artifact | Effort | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| **One person, named, saying one specific thing** (30–60s, phone) | 5 min | Instagram, LinkedIn |
| **One photo of people, not the room** | 0 min | Instagram, newsletter |
| **One sentence someone said** | 0 min | LinkedIn, newsletter |
| A photo set | already happening (Flickr) | Newsletter, site |

**The bar is deliberately low** because the failure mode is not bad artifacts, it is
zero artifacts. ODD already has a Flickr photo bank, a photographer network
(@juliustoyryla, @remushemeikka, @aes.kuva, @ferookart) and 240 Instagram posts of
proof that it can make this content. What it does not have is the rule.

## Step 3 — REACH

**Order matters, and it is not the obvious one.**

1. **The person in the artifact posts it.** Highest reach, zero cost, most credible.
2. **Ronny or a team member posts it.** 2.5–3× the brand account on every channel
   measured.
3. **The brand account posts it.** Canonical record.
4. **The newsletter collects it**, monthly, with the instruction attached.

**Repost what other people make.** `@smilefestival.de` (135 likes) and `@aes.kuva`
(114) both beat `@oddfest.co`'s own median of ~43. That content is free and better.

**Medium discipline:** Instagram carries the feeling, email carries the instruction.
Do not put the feedback form on Instagram (21 likes). Do not put pure hype in an
email with a 60% open rate.

## Step 4 — RETURN

The artifact recruits the next room. Every post about a room that happened ends with
a way into the next one — an event, an open call, a membership, a ticket.

This is where the **"comment ODD → DM the link"** mechanic belongs: it turns a post
about last week's room into DMs about next week's, and Buildspace measured it at
**14× on comments**.

---

## Who does what

The plan fails if it needs a person who does not exist. So:

| Step | Who | Time per room |
|---|---|---|
| CAPTURE | Whoever is on the door | 0 — it's a QR code |
| RECORD | Whoever is already there with a phone | 5 minutes |
| REACH 1–2 | The person in it, plus one team member | 5 minutes |
| REACH 3–4 | One named channel owner, batched weekly | 30 min/week |
| RETURN | Built into the post template | 0 |

**Total marginal cost: about 40 minutes a week**, most of it done by people who are
already in the room.

## The one number that says it is working

> **Capture rate — the percentage of people in a room who end up on the list.**

Today: unmeasured, and almost certainly under 20%. If this number does not move,
nothing else in the marketing plan matters, because the leak is bigger than anything
we can pour in.

Everything else — followers, likes, impressions — is recorded because it is cheap,
not because it is the goal.

---

## Start here

Do not roll this out across everything. Run it on **one room type** — the weekly
ODDspace event — for four weeks:

1. Print one QR code.
2. Make one artifact per event.
3. Post it in the order above.
4. Count how many people were in the room and how many joined the list.

Four data points is enough to know whether the loop works before committing
ODDfest 2027 to it.
