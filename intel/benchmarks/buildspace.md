## The four findings that carry into All Things ODD

| Finding | Number | Why it transfers |
|---|---|---|
| **"Comment X → bot DMs the link"** | 12 posts with the CTA averaged **2,791 comments** vs 196 without — **14×** | Solves the one-link-in-bio problem by moving conversion into DMs. Works at 2K followers as well as 200K, which is the rare mechanic that does. |
| **Market the people, not the programme** | Third-person hero posts about one participant averaged **13,162 likes vs 5,287** — **2.5×** | ODD has ODDspace members, ODDfest artists, Olohuone Records, the Tunement partners, the Undertow subjects. |
| **One short film per funnel stage** | 5 films totalling 9.2 minutes drew **270,560 views**; 24 lectures totalling 14.67 hours drew 135,652 | Placed at D−56, D−43, D−5, D+0, D+12 against the gate date, not sprayed. |
| **Delete your own gate** | S1 ran a rejection list; S3 said "anyone… apply — 4 mins". 500 → 30,000+ applicants | Directly relevant to ODD's open calls. |

## The corroboration that matters

Buildspace's finding that **the programme is never the subject** shows up
independently in ODD's own data. ODDletters' four worst-clicking sends are all
lineup announcements (2.00%–5.00%); its best are "Everything You Need to Know"
(9.46%) and "New Ways to Get Involved" (8.41%). Two different organisations, two
different channels, same result: people open for the news and act on the usefulness.

See [`../self/newsletter-audit.md`](../self/newsletter-audit.md) §4.

## The caution

From that teardown's own "What NOT to copy": Buildspace's posting cadence assumed
188K followers, and its free model was paid for by $3–5M/yr of sponsorship against a
125,000-person top of funnel. **Free was an outcome of that scale, not a route to
it.** The replies behaviour — 55% of early output, the part that doesn't scale — is
what built the audience. The posting cadence is what you do afterwards.
